# Capturing a physical piece

How to get a drawing, painting, print or any other made-by-hand object into Constructicon so the
digital copy is a faithful, findable record of the physical original. Worked example: an ink
drawing of a dragon that later became a logo.

## 1. Photograph or scan it

A scanner beats a camera for flat work; a camera is fine for anything bigger than the bed.

- **Flat and square.** Lay the sheet flat, shoot straight-on (camera parallel to the paper, lens
  centred), and keep the camera as far back as the frame allows, then crop. Close and angled
  gives keystoning and lens bulge.
- **Whole sheet, edges included.** Leave a margin of background around the full sheet. The
  paper edge, deckle, tape and signature are part of the piece. Crop later, never at capture.
- **Even, soft light.** Daylight from a window or two lamps at 45 degrees on either side. No flash,
  no glare on ink or graphite sheen, no shadow from your own arm. Turn off mixed colour lighting.
- **Colour card (optional).** For colour work, put a grey card or colour chart beside the sheet in
  the first frame (not in the final). It makes white balance a one-click fix.
- **Resolution.** Scanner: 600 dpi for line work up to letter size, 300 dpi for painted or
  tonal work. Camera: shoot the highest-quality JPEG or raw, at least 3000 px on the long side.
  Keep the original scan/raw; upload that, not a resized copy.
- **Hand-sized and 3D pieces** (carvings, models): see the camera notes above, plus a neutral
  backdrop and a second angle as an extra item related to the first.

## 2. Name it

Name the file before uploading, plainly and for a stranger: `dragon-original-drawing.jpg`.
Lowercase words and hyphens, no dates in the name (the date goes in the field below), and
`-scan`/`-photo` only if you keep both. Then give the item a real title (click the name on the
item page): "Dragon - original ink drawing".

## 3. Fill in the fields

On the item page, the **PHYSICAL PIECE** group appears for anything in a hobby whose
**Physical-piece fields** setting is on (the hobby page's SETTINGS group), and for any item that
already has one of these set. Press Edit:

| Field | What goes in it | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medium | What it is made with and on (start typing; earlier values are suggested) | `ink on paper` |
| Dimensions | Size of the physical piece, free text, units included | `8.5 x 11 in` |
| Date made | When it was MADE, not scanned. `2009`, `2009-06` or `2009-06-14` | `2009-06` |
| Original is | Optional: where the physical piece is now | `framed, office` |

**Date made drives the item's date.** Once set it is the date the item sorts by on the timeline
and project pages, because the scan's own date is meaningless. A date typed into the item's
Timeline date still wins over it. Clear Date made to fall back to the scan date. Leave it blank
if you truly do not know; do not guess a year.

Also fill the usual ABOUT fields: a description (what it is, what it was for, who it is of) and
tags if useful.

## 4. Provenance, hobby and project

- **Provenance: `created`.** In the ORIGIN group set Provenance to *Created* (you made it). For
  something you collected rather than made, pick the option that fits and say so in the description.
- **Hobby.** Put the item in a project that belongs to a hobby with **Physical-piece fields** switched
  on (for example a "Traditional Media" hobby). That is what makes the PHYSICAL PIECE group show up
  for new items.
- **Project.** Use the matching project (for drawings, **Drawings**). In WHERE IT LIVES, press
  Edit and add the project. Start a new project only for a series or a piece with its own story;
  a project is a shelf, not a single drawing.
- Set the project cover to the best piece if it has none.

## 5. Link it to what came from it

If the piece was reworked into something else (a logo, a vector trace, a print run, a website
header), connect them so the story is walkable in both directions:

- On the original's item page, WHERE IT LIVES, Edit, **+ Add related**, and pick each derived item.
  Relating is two-way, and it also shares tags and project membership between the two items.
- Mark derived branding files as brand assets with their role (ORIGIN, Brand asset). They then
  appear in the brand kit, and the original links to them.
- Say what happened in the description: "Scanned from the original, traced into the vector logo
  in 2017".

## Quick checklist

1. Flat, square, whole sheet, even light, 600 dpi (or best camera).
2. Plain file name, then a real title.
3. Medium, dimensions, date made (and where the original is).
4. Provenance Created; in a project of a hobby with physical-piece fields on; cover set.
5. Related to anything it became; description tells the story.
